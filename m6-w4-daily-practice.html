What the act costs
Courage in Chapters 11 to 12
⚖️ THE RULE
Atticus measures courage by what an act costs the person who does it. A brave act that costs little is not the kind he means.
WEIGH THE ACT · NOT GRADED
1. A boy dives into a pool he has dived into a hundred times. How much does it cost him?
2. A woman decides to break a habit, knowing that quitting will cause her great pain, because she wants to finish well. How much does it cost her?

Objection!: diction
A word can carry a verdict
⚖️ THE RULE
Diction is word choice. Two words can name the same trait and still feel different. If a witness uses a word that judges when a plain word would do, the objection is sustained.
OBJECTION! · NOT GRADED
Witness: “The old man was stingy with his pennies.”
Counsel: “Objection. The point is only that he spent little. ‘Stingy’ passes judgment.”
Witness: “She put her sewing down and looked out the window.”
Counsel: “Objection. These words are loaded.”
Witness: “The boy bragged about the dog.”
Counsel: “Objection. The point is only that he talked about it.”
Your diction sentences
From the lesson The Weight of a Single Word: one sentence about a person in Maycomb with a neutral word, then two rewrites, one kinder and one unkinder.
6
Objection!: pace
Long sentences slow the scene; a short one marks the turn
⚖️ THE RULE
A long sentence slows a scene. A short one speeds it up. Put one short sentence at the exact moment things turn.
OBJECTION! · NOT GRADED
Witness: “The sun sat. The dust lay. A dog slept.”
Counsel: “Objection. The witness calls this a slow, sleepy afternoon, but the sentences are all clipped.”
Witness: “The porch sat empty through a long, hot afternoon. Then a door slammed.”
Counsel: “Objection. The second sentence is too short.”
Witness: “The afternoon crept past, and the shutters stayed shut, and the dogs slept in the shade, and then a shot rang out.”
Counsel: “Objection. The turn is buried.”
Your long-then-short passage
From the lesson Pacing a Scene by Sentence Length: a quiet street that turns tense, one long sentence, then a single short sentence at the turn.
