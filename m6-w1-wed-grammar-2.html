Writing Style
TWO WORDS, OFTEN CONFUSED
VOICE = the personality on the page
The steady "sound" of the narrator: word choice, rhythm, sense of humor. You would know the voice even with the names removed. Scout's voice is plain, dry, and quietly funny.
TONE = the attitude toward this moment
How the writer feels about what is happening right now: warm, amused, tense, grave. Tone can shift scene to scene while the voice stays the same.
One narrator, one voice. Many scenes, shifting tone.
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
The same events, told in a different voice, become a different story.
Voice is the personality heard in the writing: the particular word choices, rhythms, and sense of humor that make one narrator unmistakable. Tone is the attitude the writing takes toward a given moment, and it can change from scene to scene even when the voice does not. Lee gives Scout a voice that is plain, observant, and dryly funny, the voice of a grown woman remembering her childhood with affection and honesty. That single voice carries many tones: warm when she describes summer with Jem, amused when adults act foolish, and grave when the town's harder truths break through. Hearing voice and tone separates a writer in control from one who is merely reporting.
🎯 OBJECTIVES
1
I will be able to tell the difference between voice and tone.
2
I will be able to name the tone of a passage and point to the words that create it.

📚 PART 1 · A WORKED EXAMPLE
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
Think of someone whose way of talking you could recognize anywhere, even on the phone. What makes their voice theirs?

Here is the test. Voice asks: does this sound like the same narrator throughout? Tone asks: what attitude does this particular passage take? These three sentences were written for this lesson, all in one steady voice but with different tones.

"Summer was our best season, and that summer it arrived early, smelling of cut grass and trouble." → affectionate and a little playful. Warm, fond tone.
"The new teacher had a great many ideas and not one of them fit our town." → same dry narrator, gently mocking. Amused, wry tone.
"I did not understand, then, why grown men lowered their voices when my father's name came up." → same voice, but the mood darkens. Grave, uneasy tone.

Notice the voice is constant: plain, observant, a touch dry. Only the tone moves.

⚡ PART 2 · YOUR TURN

Each line below was written for this lesson, in one narrator's voice. Name the tone of each.

⚡ TRY IT · WHAT IS THE TONE?
Choose the tone that best fits each line.
1. "Our old dog met us at the gate every evening as if we had been gone for years."
2. "He explained the whole county to us, though he had lived in it for nearly two weeks."
3. "No one spoke on the walk home; the grown-ups only watched the road and held our hands too tightly."
4. "By August the town moved so slowly that even the flies seemed to think it over."
▶  In your notebook, write two sentences about the same place in two different tones, keeping one steady voice. Then click to see an example.
Example. Warm: "The porch was where we did our best thinking, and our worst." Grave: "That same porch is where I first heard my father called names I did not understand." One voice, two tones. Yours will differ; check that the narrator still sounds like the same person.
