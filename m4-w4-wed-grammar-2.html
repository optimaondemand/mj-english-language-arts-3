Showing Who a Person Is
Writing Style Lesson
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A writer can tell a reader what a character is like, or show it and let the reader decide.
Characterization is how a writer reveals what a character is like. There are two ways to do it. Direct characterization states a trait outright: “He was cruel.” Indirect characterization shows the trait through what the character does, says, or chooses, and lets the reader judge: “He smiled as he kicked over the smaller boy’s shelter.” Skilled writers lean on the indirect kind because readers trust what they work out for themselves. One powerful tool of characterization is the foil: a character placed beside another so that the contrast sharpens both. Golding sets calm, fair-minded Ralph against fierce, hungry Jack, and steady Piggy against brutal Roger. Each pair makes the other clearer.
🎯 LESSON OBJECTIVES
1
I will be able to tell direct characterization from indirect characterization.
2
I will be able to explain what a foil is and why a writer uses one.
📋 MATERIALS
▶
Writing notebook and pencil. 5 to 10 minutes.
📚 PART 1 · A WORKED EXAMPLE
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
Which tells you more about a person: being told they are generous, or watching them quietly pay for a stranger’s meal?

Here are the two methods side by side, using sentences written for this lesson:

“Roger was cruel.” → the trait is stated outright. Direct characterization.
“Roger gathered a handful of stones and threw them just close enough to make the small boy flinch.” → the cruelty is shown and the reader judges it. Indirect characterization.
Ralph keeps the fire and the rules; Jack abandons both to hunt. → each makes the other’s nature clearer. They are foils.
⚡ PART 2 · PRACTICE

Each sentence was written for this lesson. Is it direct or indirect characterization?

⚡ TRY IT · DIRECT OR INDIRECT?
Choose the method each sentence uses.
1. Jack promised to keep the fire going, then led the hunters away the moment a pig appeared.
2. Simon was the most thoughtful boy on the island.
3. Piggy cradled the conch and reminded them that whoever held it had the right to speak.
4. Ralph was fair, and the younger boys knew it.
▶  In your notebook, take one trait (brave, selfish, kind) and write it twice: once directly, once indirectly. Click to see an example.
Trait: brave. Direct: “She was brave.” Indirect: “She stepped between the dog and the child without a word.” (The indirect sentence should show the trait through an action.)
📝 MEMORY WORK

Drill all three passages; keep the conch passage sharp.
