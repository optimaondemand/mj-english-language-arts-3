The Hints Around Simon, and the Ground Beneath Castle Rock
Literature Lesson
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A story can warn a reader about a character long before anything happens to him.
Foreshadowing, from the previous grammar lesson, appears here. As the boys move toward the far end of the island, Golding surrounds Simon with quiet hints: he wanders off alone, he speaks of going back through the dark, he sees what the others cannot. Castle Rock is the bare stone fortress where Jack sets up his own rule, the place where the gentle order of the beach gives way to fear and force. Golding marks the change through setting: open sand becomes hard rock; a meeting circle becomes a throne. The place itself argues that something has shifted.
🎯 LESSON OBJECTIVES
1
I will be able to point to specific hints that foreshadow Simon’s fate.
2
I will be able to name how the setting of Castle Rock shows the turn to tyranny.
📋 MATERIALS
▶
Lord of the Flies, Chapters 7 to 8 (own copy) and a writing notebook.
📖 PART 1 · READ, THEN TRACE THE HINTS
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
When a quiet, watchful character in a story keeps wandering off alone, what do you start to expect?

As you read, mark in your notebook every place where Simon is set apart from the others, or where a detail hints at something dark ahead.

📖 YOUR READING
Read Chapters 7 and 8 in your copy of Lord of the Flies.

One detail worth finding: at the close of Chapter 8 Golding gives the severed pig’s head a voice, calling itself the “Lord of the Flies” (Ch. 8). The phrase is a translation of an old name for a demon. Mark the moment in your own copy.

⚡ PART 2 · WHAT IS THE HINT DOING?

Each prompt below points to a kind of detail in these chapters. Choose what it foreshadows.

⚡ TRY IT · READ THE FORESHADOWING
What does each detail hint at?
1. Simon volunteers to go back through the forest in the dark, by himself.
2. The hunters reenact the kill, laughing, jabbing at a boy who plays the pig.
3. Jack leads his hunters to a fortress of bare rock at the island’s edge.
4. Alone in the clearing, Simon hears the pig’s head seem to speak to him.
📝 PART 3 · THE SETTING OF CASTLE ROCK

Castle Rock is the place where the change happens, and its setting carries the meaning.

📝 THE SETTING
Castle Rock is a bare stone platform, reached by a narrow neck of rock, with a great boulder balanced above and a high seat for the one who rules. The ground is hard and the space is closed in, the opposite of an open beach.
▶  In your notebook, write one sentence naming how Castle Rock feels different from an open beach. Then click for an example.
Example: “On the beach you could walk in any direction; on Castle Rock there is one path in, one boulder above it, and one chair at the top, so the space itself says who is in charge.” The sentence should connect the shape of the place to the kind of rule it holds.
📝 PART 4 · WORD STUDY

Jack rules Castle Rock as a tyrant. Tap to check the meaning.

⚡ TAP TO REVEAL
tyrant
Tyrant means a ruler who holds power through fear and force rather than law or consent. A tyrant answers to no one and keeps order by punishment. The word names exactly what Jack becomes on Castle Rock.
🌱 WORD ROOTS
Tyrant comes from Greek tyrannos, a ruler who seized power rather than inheriting it. The Greeks did not always mean it as an insult; the harsher sense we carry today is what centuries of tyrants made of the word.
📝 WORD STUDY · PRACTICE
Write a sentence describing Jack’s rule using tyrant. Then rewrite it twice: once with a synonym, once expanding it (“a tyrant who…”). Look tyrant up in a dictionary and record three things: how the word breaks into syllables, how it is said aloud, and what part of speech it is.
📝 MEMORY WORK

Drill all three passages this week and say the conch passage aloud once more.
