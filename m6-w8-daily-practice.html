Objection!: metaphor and simile
Pictures that carry an argument
⚖️ THE RULE
A metaphor says one thing is another, with no like or as. A simile compares with like or as. A plain statement makes no comparison.
OBJECTION! · METAPHOR AND SIMILE · NOT GRADED
Testimony: “The crowd was a river pouring out of the courthouse.” The witness says this is a simile. Ruling?
Which sentence is a simile?
Testimony: “The judge entered the room and took his seat.” The witness says this is a metaphor. Ruling?
Your metaphor and your simile
From the lesson Pictures That Carry an Argument. Label each one. The metaphor uses no like or as; the simile does.

Objection!: tone and pacing
Speed and mood, on purpose
⚖️ THE RULE
Short, clipped sentences speed a passage up. Long, flowing sentences slow it down. A tone shift is a turn in attitude inside a passage.
OBJECTION! · TONE AND PACING · NOT GRADED
Testimony: “He stopped. He looked. He ran.” The witness says these short sentences speed the pace up. Ruling?
Testimony: adding long, flowing detail to a sentence makes a passage move faster. Ruling?
Which sentence shifts the tone from calm to uneasy?
