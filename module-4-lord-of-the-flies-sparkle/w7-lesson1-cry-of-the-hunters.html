Written 1880 · Lines 1–9 of 15

Márgarét, are you gríeving Over Goldengrove unleaving? Leáves, líke the things of man, you With your fresh thoughts care for, can you? Áh! ás the heart grows older It will come to such sights colder By and by, nor spare a sigh Though worlds of wanwood leafmeal lie; And yet you wíll weep and know why.
Goldengrove: a wood of trees turned gold in autumn; Hopkins’s name for it · unleaving: losing its leaves · wanwood: pale, faded woods; a word Hopkins made · leafmeal: leaf by leaf, fallen leaves broken to bits; Hopkins’s word, like piecemeal · the accent marks: Hopkins’s marks for the syllables to stress, as in Márgarét

Professor Lovelore

Good. Here is the word for the day.

Resolution: the part of a story after the climax, where the conflict ends and we see what it has come to.

Golding ends the hunt in one short sentence, the moment the cry stops: “The ululation faltered and died away.” (Ch. 12) But look at what his resolution leaves open, out there on the water.
