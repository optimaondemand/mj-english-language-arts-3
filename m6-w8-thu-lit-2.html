What Conscience Asks After the Verdict
Literature Lesson
⚖️ THE CENTRAL QUESTION, AFTER THE VERDICT
What does justice ask of the few who serve it, once the verdict has already gone the other way?
THE COMMUNITY
Much of Maycomb absorbs the verdict and moves on, as if the matter is settled.
THE FEW
A handful of people still carry the weight of what conscience demanded of them.
📖 FOCUS
A verdict ends a trial, but it does not end the demands of conscience.
The previous lesson covered the aftermath. This lesson looks at how it deepens the question this module has lived with: what justice costs, and what it asks of the few who serve it. Once the verdict is in, the community has a choice about how much to remember and how much to let go. Much of the town settles back into ordinary life. A smaller number of people cannot set it down so easily; their conscience keeps asking something of them even after the official decision is made. Lee shows both responses without lecturing the reader. The larger debate about the novel’s place and reception comes in a later lesson. This lesson traces, from the text, who carries the weight of conscience after the verdict, and how the community around them responds.
🎯 OBJECTIVES
1
I will be able to describe how the town and how individual characters respond to the verdict.
2
I will be able to explain how the aftermath sharpens the question of what justice asks of the few who serve it.
📋 MATERIALS
▶
Own copy of To Kill a Mockingbird and writing notebook. Chapters 25 to 28 are reread.
📖 PART 1 · WHO CARRIES THE WEIGHT
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
Think of a time you did the right thing even though it changed nothing in the moment. What did it ask of you?

Skim back through Chapters 25 to 28. In the notebook, jot down who, in these chapters, keeps carrying the weight of the trial, and who seems ready to set it down.

📝 BEFORE SORTING
A claim about a character should rest on what the text actually shows, not on what we wish a character felt. In the sorting below, ask: does the novel give evidence for this, or is a blank being filled in? The larger debate about the novel's reputation is for a later lesson; stay with the text.
⚡ PART 2 · GROUNDED OR NOT?

For each statement, decide whether it is grounded in what Chapters 25 to 28 actually show, or whether it goes beyond what the text supports.

⚡ TRY IT · DOES THE TEXT SUPPORT THIS?
Choose the best label for each statement.
1. After the verdict, much of Maycomb tries to return to its ordinary routine.
2. By these chapters, the entire town secretly regrets the verdict and wishes it undone.
3. The verdict ends the trial, but the consequences of it keep unfolding in the chapters that follow.
4. By the end of these chapters, every character has fully changed their view of justice.
📝 PART 3 · WORD STUDY

Standing by a hard duty after the verdict is a matter of integrity. Tap to check the meaning.

⚡ TAP TO REVEAL
integrity
Integrity is wholeness of character: acting on the same principles when they cost something as when they cost nothing. After a lost verdict, conscience asks for integrity rather than victory, because nothing outward is left to reward it.
🌱 WORD ROOTS
Integrity comes from Latin integer, “whole and untouched.” The same wholeness counts in integer, a whole number, and entire. A person of integrity is undivided.
📝 WORD STUDY · PRACTICE
Write a sentence using integrity. Then rewrite it twice: once with a synonym, once expanding it ("an integrity that…"). Look integrity up in a dictionary and record three things: how the word breaks into syllables, how it is said aloud, and what part of speech it is.
