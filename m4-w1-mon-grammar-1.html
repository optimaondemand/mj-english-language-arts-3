📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Imagery gives a reader a place through the five senses.
Imagery is language that appeals to the senses: sight, sound, smell, taste, and touch. The phrase “the sand was hot” makes a reader feel heat; “a wall of green heat shimmered above the beach” makes a reader see the air bend. Imagery is a choice. A writer picks which senses to wake up and which details to give, and that choice shapes how a place feels. In Lord of the Flies, William Golding fills the opening of the island with heat, glare, and dense green.
🎯 LESSON OBJECTIVES
1
I will be able to identify imagery and name which sense it appeals to.
2
I will be able to write a sentence that uses imagery from more than one sense.
📋 MATERIALS
▶
Writing notebook and pencil. 5 to 10 minutes.
📚 PART 1 · A WORKED EXAMPLE
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
Which of these makes a beach feel more real?

Imagery wakes up one of five senses. Each sentence below was written for this lesson.

Sight: “Green light pressed down through the leaves.” → sight
Sound: “Far off, the surf thudded like a slow drum.” → sound
Touch: “The sand burned the soles of his bare feet.” → touch
Smell: “The air was thick with the smell of rotting fruit.” → smell
Taste: “Salt dried on his lips, bitter and gritty.” → taste

⚡ TRY IT · NAME THE SENSE
Which sense does each sentence appeal to?
1. A bird cried out once, and the sound bounced back from the rocks.
2. The heat lay heavy on his shoulders, and sweat ran down his back.
3. Pink granite rose in broken slabs against a hard blue sky.
4. The wind carried the sharp green smell of crushed leaves.
