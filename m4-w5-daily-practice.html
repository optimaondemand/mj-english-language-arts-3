Two features of the style
What the long sentences and the images do
🔥 THE RULE
Golding slows the paragraph with long, calm sentences. He also sets beautiful images at the hardest moment. The calm makes the loss feel larger, not smaller.
SPOT THE FEATURE · NOT GRADED
1. “The tide came in slowly, patient and silver, and the dark water folded around the still shape on the sand.” Which feature does it show?
2. “Soft lamplight lay across the floor while the news broke his heart.” Which feature does it show?
3. Which sentence does NOT use either feature?

Signal Fire Sentences: four shapes
Mix them to control pace
🔥 THE RULE
A simple sentence has one independent clause. A compound sentence joins two with and, but, or so. A complex sentence adds a dependent clause. A compound-complex sentence has two independent clauses and a dependent one.
FEED THE FIRE · NOT GRADED
1. The fire sank low, and the boys moved closer.
2. When the chant stopped, the island seemed to listen.
3. Because the tide was rising, the boys left the rocks, and Ralph stayed.
4. The sand held the print of one bare foot.
One idea in three shapes
From the lesson Sentence Variety: Four Shapes: the same idea as a simple, a compound, and a complex sentence.

6
Signal Fire Sentences: loose and periodic
Where the weight falls
🔥 THE RULE
A loose sentence gives its main idea first, then adds details. A periodic sentence holds the main idea until the very end. Loose feels natural. Periodic feels deliberate.
FEED THE FIRE · NOT GRADED
1. The conch lay in the dust, cracked and pale, while the wind moved over the empty platform.
2. Cracked and pale, in the dust of the empty platform, the conch lay.
3. Which sentence is periodic?
A loose sentence, then a periodic one
From the lesson Where the Weight Falls. Write a loose sentence, then rewrite it as a periodic one.
