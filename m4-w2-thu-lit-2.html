Marking the Tone Shifts in Chapter 4
Literature Lesson
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Chapter 4 does not stay in one feeling. It slides from morning play toward something darker.
Tone and mood from the previous grammar lesson apply to a single chapter. Chapter 4 opens in the ordinary rhythm of island days, almost peaceful, then turns: the littluns are bullied, the hunters chant, the fire is let die, and a ship passes unseen. Golding shifts the tone across the chapter from light to grim, and that shift is the first real crack in the boys' order.
🎯 LESSON OBJECTIVES
1
I will be able to mark where the tone shifts across Chapter 4 and name the feeling at each stage.
2
I will be able to explain what the conch, the fire, and the painted face each stand for.
📋 MATERIALS
▶
Lord of the Flies, Chapter 4 (own copy) and a writing notebook.
📖 PART 1 · FOLLOW THE SHIFT
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
A scene can begin happy and end frightening. What makes a reader feel the change?

In your notebook, write down three moments in order and name the tone at each: where it begins, where it turns, and where it ends.

📖 YOUR READING
Reread Chapter 4 in your copy of Lord of the Flies.
⚡ PART 2 · PLACE THE TONE

Each moment below is from Chapter 4. Place its tone on the scale from light to grim.

⚡ TRY IT · LIGHT OR GRIM?
How does the tone feel at each moment?
1. The boys move through an ordinary island morning.
2. Roger throws stones to miss, the old rules still holding him back, but barely.
3. The fire has gone out, and a ship passes on the horizon without seeing them.
✏️ PART 3 · LABEL THE SYMBOLS

Chapter 4 shows what each island symbol stands for.

✏️ NOTEBOOK TASK
In your notebook, list three things: the conch, the signal fire, and the painted face. Beside each, write one short note about what it stands for.
📝 PART 4 · WORD STUDY

When the boys let the fire die, they were negligent. Tap to check the meaning.

⚡ TAP TO REVEAL
negligent
Negligent means failing to take proper care of something you are responsible for. The hunters were negligent with the fire, and that neglect cost the boys their chance of rescue.
🌱 WORD ROOTS
Negligent comes from Latin neglegere, built from nec, “not,” and legere, “to pick up or choose.” To be negligent is literally to not pick a thing up. The same legere appears in collect and select.
📝 WORD STUDY · PRACTICE
Write a sentence using negligent about the hunters and the fire. Then rewrite it twice: once with a synonym, once expanding it (“negligent in a way that…”). Look negligent up in a dictionary and record three things: how the word breaks into syllables, how it is said aloud, and what part of speech it is.
📝 MEMORY WORK

Drill Passage 1, the conch passage, and keep learning Passage 2, Simon's death.
