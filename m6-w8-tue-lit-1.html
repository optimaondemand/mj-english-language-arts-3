📖 FOCUS
The verdict is in, but the story is not finished with its consequences.
Read Chapters 25 to 28 in one sitting. The aftermath of the trial settles over Maycomb. The town tries to return to ordinary life, neighbors talk and the seasons turn, while the consequences of the trial keep unfolding underneath the calm. The year moves into autumn, and the children prepare for a school pageant. Lee keeps her voice steady through all of it. The task is to read these chapters straight through and then retell what happens in own words. Telling a section plainly is how a careful reader makes sure of what happened before beginning to interpret it.
🎯 OBJECTIVES
1
I will be able to retell the events of Chapters 25 to 28 in order and in my own words.
2
I will be able to describe how the town's ordinary surface sits over the unfinished consequences of the trial.
📋 MATERIALS
▶
Own copy of To Kill a Mockingbird and writing notebook. About 30 minutes.
📖 PART 1 · READ CHAPTERS 25 TO 28
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
Before you read, set an expectation. The trial is over and the verdict is in. What do you think the town does next?

Read Chapters 25 to 28 in one sitting, without stopping to take notes. Let the chapters carry the reader to the edge of the ending before going back to think about them.

📖 READING
Read Chapters 25 to 28 in your copy of To Kill a Mockingbird. This novel is still under copyright, so the text is not reproduced here.
⚡ PART 2 · PUT THE EVENTS IN ORDER

A plain retell means getting the order right. For each pair, choose what comes first across Chapters 25 to 28.

⚡ TRY IT · WHAT HAPPENS FIRST?
Which event comes earlier in Chapters 25 to 28?
1. The town settles back into its ordinary routine, or the children's school pageant arrives?
2. The aftermath of the trial works through the town, or the year turns toward autumn?
3. The night of the pageant, or the stretch of ordinary days that comes before it?
▶  In your notebook, retell Chapters 25 to 28 in four or five sentences, in order, in your own words. Click for what a strong retell includes.
A strong retell names the aftermath of the trial, the town's effort to return to ordinary life, the consequences that keep moving underneath, the turn of the year toward autumn, and the approach of the school pageant that ends this stretch. Keep it plain. Do not interpret yet; just say what happens.
📝 PART 3 · WORD STUDY

After the trial, much of Maycomb returns to a smooth, settled complacency. Tap to check the meaning.

⚡ TAP TO REVEAL
complacency
Complacency is a quiet, untroubled satisfaction with how things are: a comfort that keeps a person from looking closely at a problem. In these chapters the town's complacency lets it move on while the harm of the trial is still unsettled.
🌱 WORD ROOTS
Complacency comes from Latin complacere, from com, an intensifier, and placere, “to please.” Complacency is being so pleased with things that one stops looking at them. The same placere pleases placid and pleasant.
📝 WORD STUDY · PRACTICE
Write a sentence using complacency. Then rewrite it twice: once with a synonym, once expanding it ("a complacency that…"). Look complacency up in a dictionary and record three things: how the word breaks into syllables, how it is said aloud, and what part of speech it is.
