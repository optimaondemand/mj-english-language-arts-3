The Mad Dog and a Town’s Idea of Courage
Literature Lesson
🎯 TWO PICTURES OF COURAGE IN CHAPTER 10
WHAT MAYCOMB ADMIRES
A steady hand and a clean shot. The town suddenly sees Atticus as brave when he drops the mad dog with one rifle bullet.
Courage as visible, public, easy to cheer.
WHAT ATTICUS LIVES
He had put the rifle away years ago and does not boast of it. The courage he prizes is the quiet kind the town does not notice.
Courage as private, unshowy, easy to miss.
The chapter sets the town’s loud idea of bravery beside Atticus’s quiet one.
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A scene can argue about an idea without ever stating the argument.
In Chapter 10, Scout is ashamed of her father. Atticus seems older and duller than other fathers; he does not hunt or play football, and he tells the children it is a sin to kill a mockingbird. Then a mad dog comes down the street, and Atticus, to Scout’s astonishment, takes the sheriff’s rifle and kills it with a single shot. The town admires him at once. But Lee is doing something quieter underneath. The dog scene gives Maycomb a courage it can see and cheer for, while the courage Atticus actually values, the kind that will carry him through the trial ahead, stays out of sight. The task: read the chapter and retell what happens, then notice the gap between the bravery the town praises and the bravery Atticus practices.
🎯 OBJECTIVES
1
I will be able to retell the mad-dog scene in order and in my own words.
2
I will be able to describe the difference between the courage the town admires and the courage Atticus lives.
📋 MATERIALS
▶
Own copy of To Kill a Mockingbird (Chapter 10) and a writing notebook.
📖 PART 1 · READ CHAPTER 10
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
Before you read, think of someone you admire for a reason most people would not notice. What is the quiet thing they do?

Read Chapter 10 in one sitting. Watch for the moment the town’s view of Atticus flips, and for what the children learn that the town misses.

📖 YOUR READING
Read all of Chapter 10 in your own copy. This novel is under copyright, so the text is not reproduced here.
⚡ PART 2 · PUT THE EVENTS IN ORDER

A plain retell means getting the order right. For each pair, choose what comes first in the chapter.

⚡ TRY IT · WHAT HAPPENS FIRST?
Which event comes earlier in Chapter 10?
1. The mad dog comes down the street, or Scout feels ashamed that her father seems dull?
2. The sheriff offers Atticus the rifle, or Atticus takes the single shot?
3. The children learn their father’s old reputation as a marksman, or the dog is shot?
▶  In your notebook, retell the mad-dog scene in four or five sentences, in order, in your own words. Then click for a guide to what a strong retell includes.
A strong retell names Scout’s embarrassment with her father, the arrival of the mad dog, the sheriff handing Atticus the rifle, the single shot that drops the dog, and the children’s shock at learning their father was once a famous marksman. Keep it plain. Do not interpret yet; just say what happens.
📝 PART 3 · WORD STUDY

Atticus does not parade his skill; he keeps it unassuming. Tap to check the meaning, then practice the word several ways.

⚡ TAP TO REVEAL
unassuming
Unassuming describes someone modest and quiet about their own ability, not eager to be noticed or praised. Atticus is unassuming about the very skill the town suddenly admires, and that modesty is part of what the children come to respect.
🌱 WORD ROOTS
Unassuming is built on Latin ad, “to,” and sumere, “to take up.” An unassuming person does not take up airs, or space, or credit. The same sumere is taken up in consume and resume.
📝 WORD STUDY
Write a sentence using unassuming. Then rewrite it twice: once with a synonym, once expanding it ("an unassuming way that…"). Look unassuming up in a dictionary and record three things: how the word breaks into syllables, how it is said aloud, and what part of speech it is.
