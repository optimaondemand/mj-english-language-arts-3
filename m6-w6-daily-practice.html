Objection!: polysyndeton and asyndeton
Pile the “and”s up, or strip them away
⚖️ THE RULE
Polysyndeton repeats “and” between every part, so the sentence slows and weighs down. Asyndeton drops the “and”s and uses commas, so the sentence speeds up.
OBJECTION! · NOT GRADED
Witness: “She stood, nodded, turned, left.”
Counsel: “Objection. The witness calls this polysyndeton, but no ‘and’ appears.”
Witness: “The fans turned and the clock ticked and the jurors shifted and nobody spoke.”
Counsel: “Objection. This is only a plain sentence.”
Witness: “The clerk read the next name.”
Counsel: “Objection. The witness calls this asyndeton, but there is no list.”
Which revision speeds up He rose and he spoke and he sat and he waited.?
Your two lists
From the lesson Conjunctions That Change the Speed: one list of four actions with polysyndeton, then the same list with asyndeton.
5
Objection!: symbols
A real thing that stands for a larger idea
⚖️ THE RULE
A symbol is a real thing the story keeps returning to and loads with a larger idea. A detail that only describes the scene is not a symbol.
OBJECTION! · NOT GRADED
Witness: “A fan lay on the bench, and a woman used it to cool herself.”
Counsel: “Objection. The witness calls the fan a symbol, but it only describes the room.”
Witness: “A lantern hung by the door, lit whenever someone told the truth.”
Counsel: “Objection. It is just a lantern.”
Witness: “A cracked teacup sat on the shelf, and the narrator never mentioned it again.”
Counsel: “Objection. This is not a symbol.”
Your symbol sentences
From the lesson When a Thing Stands for an Idea: one ordinary object in a Maycomb scene, one sentence introducing it plainly, and one where it carries a larger meaning.
