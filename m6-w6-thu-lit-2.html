📖 FOCUS
Good writing is rewriting, and a strong essay starts with a strong question.
The lesson brings the module’s work together in two steps. First, the Creative Scene is revised using a classmate’s feedback. A reader can point out where a scene lost the voice, where a detail was thin, or where the pace dragged, things the writer cannot easily see. Second, the Final Essay is framed, not drafted. The evolving answer to the central question becomes a clear thesis: a single sentence that states what justice costs in Maycomb and what it asks of the few who serve it. A short outline then shows where the Analysis Project, the Creative Scene, and the textual evidence will each support that thesis. A clear thesis and a real plan make the essay itself far easier to write.
🎯 OBJECTIVES
1
I will be able to revise my Creative Scene using specific feedback from a reader.
2
I will be able to write a clear thesis for the Final Essay and outline the evidence that supports it.
📋 MATERIALS
▶
Creative Scene draft with a classmate’s feedback, Analysis Project, and a Word document. About 40 minutes.
⚡ PART 1 · REVISE WITH A READER’S EYE
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
When a reader gives you feedback, which response usually makes your writing better?

Strong feedback is specific and kind. For each comment a reader might give, decide how useful it is for revision, then read why.

⚡ TRY IT · HOW USEFUL IS THIS FEEDBACK?
For each comment, choose how useful it is for revising a scene.
1. "In the third paragraph the voice stopped sounding like Scout and started sounding like an adult explaining."
2. "I liked it. Good job."
3. "Somewhere in the middle it felt slow to me."
✏️ NOW REVISE
Take the classmate’s most specific note and make one real change to the scene: sharpen the voice, add a concrete detail, or fix the pace with sentence length or conjunctions. Keep both versions to compare.
📝 PART 2 · SHAPE YOUR THESIS AND OUTLINE

Frame the Final Essay: sharpen the evolving answer to the central question into one thesis sentence, then sketch where each project and piece of evidence will go. Work in the Word document.

A WORKING SHAPE FOR THE OUTLINE
Thesis: one sentence answering "What does justice cost in Maycomb, and what does it ask of the few who serve it?"
Body 1: evidence from the Analysis Project (how one chapter builds the cost of justice).
Body 2: a moment from the trial chapters, with a short quotation from the novel.
Body 3: what the Creative Scene shows about the same idea.
Objections: two strong objections to the thesis, each answered.

The full essay is written later in the module. This lesson needs only a clear thesis and this outline.

📝 PART 3 · WORD STUDY

A thesis is only as strong as the evidence that corroborates it. Tap to check the meaning.

⚡ TAP TO REVEAL
corroborate
Corroborate means to strengthen a claim with independent support. One detail can suggest something; two or three that agree corroborate it. An essay needs evidence that corroborates the thesis, not evidence that merely repeats it.
🌱 WORD ROOTS
Corroborate comes from Latin corroborare, built on robur, “oak” and so “strength.” The same oak stands in robust. To corroborate a claim is to give it the strength of hardwood.
📝 WORD STUDY · PRACTICE
Write a sentence using corroborate. Then rewrite it twice: once with a synonym, once expanding it ("evidence that corroborates…"). Look corroborate up in a dictionary and record three things: how the word breaks into syllables, how it is said aloud, and what part of speech it is.
💪 MEMORY WORK

Continue the closing line from your own copy.
