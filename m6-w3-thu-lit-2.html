What Real Courage Is
Literature Lesson
WHAT COURAGE IS, AND IS NOT
NOT THIS
Physical strength, or a weapon in hand. Power that makes winning easy. Scout first thinks this is what bravery looks like.
REAL COURAGE
Holding to what is right even when you expect to lose, and carrying it through to the end.
Atticus puts this into words for Scout. Lee shows it in action all around her.
Scout is told what courage is once, but she learns it by watching the adults around her.
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A book can name a virtue once and then spend the rest of its pages showing it to you.
In these chapters Lee teaches Scout, and the reader, what courage really is. At one point Atticus tells Scout that real courage is not physical strength but the resolve to do what is right even when a loss is expected, and to carry it through. That line is a piece of direct characterization: Atticus states the trait outright. Lee does not stop there. She surrounds the statement with indirect characterization: courage shown in action, in Atticus taking a case the town will hate, in a neighbor facing a fire or a long fight, while Scout works out the meaning by watching. Lee builds the idea of courage from both directions, the told and the shown, and Scout comes to understand it indirectly, the same way the reader does.

📖 PART 1 · TOLD AND SHOWN
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
Before this lesson, if someone said "that took courage," what picture came to mind?

Lee works the idea of courage from two directions at once. Sometimes a character names it outright (direct). More often she lets us watch courage happen and name it ourselves (indirect). The examples below were written for this lesson; none are from the novel.

"Courage isn't a man with a rifle," he told the girl. "It's finishing what's right when you know it will cost you." → a character names the trait. Direct characterization of courage.
He signed his name to the unpopular case, folded the paper, and walked home past the men who would no longer meet his eye. → we watch the act and call it courage. Indirect, through action.
The girl watched her father all that week and slowly understood what he had meant. → the lesson lands by watching, not by lecture. Indirect, the way Scout learns.
⚡ PART 2 · YOUR TURN

Each sentence below was written for this lesson. Decide whether it characterizes courage directly or indirectly.

⚡ TRY IT · DIRECT OR INDIRECT?
Choose the best label for each sentence.
1. The whole porch had gone quiet, but she kept her seat and finished what she had come to say.
2. He was the bravest man the children would ever know.
3. She did not understand it that night, but after watching him all week she finally did.
4. "Real courage," he said, "is doing right when you are sure to lose."
▶  In your notebook, name one moment in Chapters 7 to 9 where you watched courage happen rather than being told about it, and say in one sentence how you knew it was courage. Then click for a guide.
A strong answer points to an action and names the cost: Atticus agreeing to defend Tom Robinson when he knows the town will turn against him and his children, or a neighbor facing the loss of a home with steadiness. You knew it was courage because the person kept doing the right thing even when it was sure to cost them something. That is indirect characterization, and it is how Scout learns the lesson too.
📝 PART 3 · WORD STUDY

Atticus keeps an outward tranquility even as the town turns against him. Tap to check the meaning, then practice the word several ways.

⚡ TAP TO REVEAL
tranquility
Tranquility is a state of calm and quiet steadiness. Atticus meets insults and pressure with tranquility, and that calm is part of how Lee shows his courage: he does not need to shout to do the hard, right thing.
🌱 WORD ROOTS
Tranquility comes from Latin tranquillus, “calm,” used of a sea without waves. The word keeps its water: tranquility is the stillness left when the surface stops moving.
📝 WORD STUDY
Write a sentence using tranquility. Then rewrite it twice: once with a synonym, once expanding it ("a tranquility that…"). Look tranquility up in a dictionary and record three things: how the word breaks into syllables, how it is said aloud, and what part of speech it is.
