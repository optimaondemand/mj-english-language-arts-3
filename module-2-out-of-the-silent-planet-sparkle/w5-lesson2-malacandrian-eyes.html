Professor Lovelore

Well done. All through these chapters you knew what Weston did not: that the voice was real, that the roar was laughter, and that the old hross he shouted at was fast asleep.

When the reader knows something a character does not, that is dramatic irony. Lewis uses it so you can judge Weston before the Oyarsa does, and then wonder whether you would have seen any better.

You have watched Weston from outside. Now hear his speech beside Ransom’s version.
