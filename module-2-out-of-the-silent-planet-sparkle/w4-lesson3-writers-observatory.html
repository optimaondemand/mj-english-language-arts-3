2
✏️ Step 1 · Learn
Tap the whole phrase.

A gerund is a verb + -ing used as a noun. A gerund phrase is the gerund and its words, working together as one noun: a subject, a direct object, or the object of a preposition. An infinitive is to + a verb. An infinitive phrase works as a noun, an adjective, or an adverb, often telling why. Two tests settle most cases.

KindTestExample
gerund phraseSwap the phrase for it or something.Climbing the road nearly killed Ransom.
infinitive phraseThe word right after to is a verb.Augray offered to carry him.
Look-alikeWhat it really is
Gasping for breath, he kept going.A participial phrase: it describes he.
He was climbing.A progressive verb: the sentence’s own verb.
He walked to the cave.to + a noun: a prepositional phrase.

The snow did not crunch under Tess’s boots. It squeaked, like a door in an empty house, and every squeak went out across the ridge and did not come back. Her helmet smelled of warm plastic and her own breath. Far below the ice, something glowed a slow and patient blue. Mapping the ridge was her whole job. On it stood the pillars: forty of them, taller than the lander, every one turned toward the same dark edge of the sky. Machines, she thought. Somebody built these and left. She raised her camera to map them, and the nearest pillar turned from white to the faint pink of a bruise.
