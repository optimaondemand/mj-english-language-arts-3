Clause

A clause has its own subject and its own verb. An adverb clause opens with a joining word and does an adverb’s job.
It tells when
When the rain began, Ransom knew he was home.
It tells why
Ransom kept his eyes shut because the light was blinding.
It tells in spite of what
Although Weston planned the voyage, Devine took the controls.
It tells under what condition
If the voyage lasted past ninety days, the ship would unbody.
Rule one: the comma
Clause first? Put a comma after it.,
Rule two: it cannot stand alone
Because the air was bad. That is a fragment.
2
✏️ Step 1 · Learn
Tap the whole adverb clause.

An adverb clause opens with a joining word such as when, before, after, while, until, because, since, although, if, or as if. Like an adverb, it tells when, why, in spite of what, under what condition, or how. Unlike a single adverb, it has its own subject and verb.

Your editing pass: one page of your story, three fixes
  1. Paste a passage from your story, about a page.
  2. Read it aloud. Where your voice runs out of breath, look for a run-on or a comma splice. Show it before and after.

About one page · your own words · before and after for each fix
🔎 Test your fix

Run-on: two sentences with nothing between them. Comma splice: two sentences with only a comma. Fragment: a clause that cannot stand alone. An adverb clause that opens a sentence needs a comma after it.

💡 Show a starter

Before: [the sentence as it was]. After: [the same sentence, fixed]. What I changed: ___.

📌 Watch how
Jonah’s editing pass
Jonah · 8th Grade Scholar

Run-on or splice. Before: Tess hummed to keep warm, the pillar turned pink. After: When Tess hummed to keep warm, the pillar turned pink.

Fragment. Before: Because the radio was dead. After: Because the radio was dead, Tess had no one to ask.

Adverb clause join. Before: The snow squeaked. Tess stopped walking. After: Tess stopped walking whenever the snow squeaked.

The passage, edited. Because the radio was dead, Tess had no one to ask. The snow squeaked like a door in an empty house, so she stopped walking whenever it did. Inside her helmet the air smelled of warm plastic and her own breath. When she hummed to keep warm, the nearest pillar turned pink, the faint pink of a bruise.
