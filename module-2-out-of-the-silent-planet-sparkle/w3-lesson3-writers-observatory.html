✏️ Step 1 · Learn
Tap the phrase, then name it.

A phrase is a group of words with no subject and verb of its own. Three kinds do big work in a sentence.

KindJobExample
prepositionalpreposition + its object; tells where, when, how, which one or whyacross the lake · for warmth
appositivea noun phrase beside a noun that renames it; commas on both sides when it is extraHnohra, a grey-muzzled hross, taught Ransom.
participiala verb form ending in -ing, -ed or -en, plus its words, acting as an adjective; an opener takes a commaPaddling across the lake, the hross watched.
⚠️

Not every -ing word is a participle. In “was talking,” the word is the main verb. And to + a verb is an infinitive, a different kind of phrase.

⚖️ Plain or Loaded?
Same meaning, different feeling.

The same thing can be named in a plain way or a loaded way. Two words can share a dictionary meaning and still carry different feelings, like curious and nosy.

Write two pairs of your own. In each pair, one word is plain and one is loaded, with the same dictionary meaning. Then add one line on the feeling each loaded word carries.

Jonah’s opening
Jonah · 8th Grade Scholar

The lander settled onto the ice with a groan, and the radio went quiet. Tess, the survey team’s only mapmaker, waited for the static to return. It never did. Kneeling at the hatch, she pressed one glove into the snow. It squeaked, thin and bright, like a door in an empty house. The air in her helmet tasted of metal and warm rubber. Far under the ice, a pale blue light moved slowly, as if something down there were turning over in its sleep. She told herself it was only a trick of the cold. Then she stood and looked along the ridge. Pale shapes rose from the snow in a long line, too tall and too straight to be rock. Tess wrote the time in her notebook: noon, by a clock that meant nothing here. Her hands were steady, but her handwriting was not. Nobody knew where she was. Nobody would hear if she called. And still, standing in the silence, she felt the strangest wish to walk toward them.
