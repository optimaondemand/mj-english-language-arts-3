For tho’ from out our bourne of Time and Place The flood may bear me far, I hope to see my Pilot face to face When I have crost the bar.

📖 Write the Preface
Your world, your claim, your evidence
📌 A model preface
Jonah
8th Grade Scholar

My world: Tess maps a silent ice moon where forty pillars stand on a ridge, all facing the same dark edge of the sky.

The detail I’m proudest of: Snow that squeaks like a door in an empty house.

My claim: When we assume we understand a stranger, we lose the chance to learn what they actually are, and we risk hurting them.

Evidence 1 (Ch. 8): Ransom calls the sorns “Spooks on stilts” before he knows anything about them; his fear comes from old stories, not from the sorns.

Evidence 2 (Ch. 21): From the ship he sees the handramits are engineered, not natural, and his whole picture of Malacandra turns out to be “parochial.”

From my story: …when Tess decides the pillars are abandoned machines and almost misses that one of them is answering her.

A reader objects: Sometimes assuming the worst keeps you alive.

My answer: Ransom’s fear of the sorns came from old stories, and then the sorn Augray gave him food and led him across the high plateau (Ch. 15 and 16). Caution is not the same as deciding before you look.
