📖 FOCUS
A small idea, planted in the right ground, can grow a whole story.
The task is to plan an original Jackson-style story. The heart of "The Lottery" is a tradition no one questions, hidden inside an ordinary day. The story needs an invented tradition and an ordinary world to bury it in. A grim ending like Jackson’s is not required; what is required is a custom whose calm surface hides something the reader slowly senses. The seed roller below supplies a random world, place, and tradition: keep it, roll again, or reshape it. Then sketch a short outline.
🎯 LESSON OBJECTIVES
1
I will be able to invent an ordinary world that hides one unsettling tradition.
2
I will be able to sketch a short outline with a buried hint and a deliberate blank.

✏️ PART 2 · OUTLINE THE STORY

In the notebook, sketch a short outline of four or five lines. Use the seed, and plan in the three Jackson tools.

1. The ordinary world. Where and when does the day begin? Keep it calm.
2. The tradition. What custom is being carried out, and how routine does it feel to the people?
3. The buried hint. What small, easily-missed detail will point toward the turn?
4. The turn. What changes, or is revealed, near the end?
5. The blank. What is deliberately never explained?
▶  Write your five-line outline first. Then click to see a sample outline built from a seed.
Sample (from the seed: small mountain town, the public library, the yearly "returning of the books"): 1. A bright autumn Saturday; townspeople file into the old library. 2. Once a year every family returns one book, chosen by the head librarian. 3. Buried hint: a child asks which book her family must return, and her mother goes quiet. 4. Turn: the returned books are never reshelved; they are taken to a locked room. 5. Blank: why the room exists, and what happens to the books, is never explained. Your outline will differ; that is the point.
