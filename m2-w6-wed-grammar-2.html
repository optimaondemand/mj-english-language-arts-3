Lesson 2.28
The Editing Pass: Clean, Clear Sentences
Grammar Lesson
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Editing is reading your own sentences one at a time, slowly, to see what they actually say.
Revising fixes the story; editing fixes the sentences. Today you make an editing pass through your draft, looking for three problems that hide easily. A run-on jams two complete sentences together with no punctuation or joining word. A fragment is a piece that is missing a subject or a verb, so it cannot stand alone. A comma splice joins two complete sentences with only a comma. You learned this week that an adverb clause can join two events on purpose; an editing pass catches the times two events ran together by accident. Read each sentence aloud. If your voice runs out of breath or stops too soon, you have found one to fix.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to spot run-ons, fragments, and comma splices.
2
I will be able to make a clean editing pass through my own draft.
📋 WHAT YOU NEED FOR THIS LESSON
▶
Your story draft, open and ready to edit, your writing notebook, and a pencil. About 5 to 10 minutes of practice plus your own editing pass.
📚 PART 1 · A WORKED EXAMPLE
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
When you read your own writing, do you read it silently or aloud? (No wrong answer.)

Here is each problem and a clean fix. Notice that the fix is usually a period, a joining word, or an adverb clause.

Run-on: “The hatch opened the cold rushed in.” Fix: “When the hatch opened, the cold rushed in.”
Fragment: “Standing at the edge of the ridge.” Fix: “She stood at the edge of the ridge.”
Comma splice: “The wind fell silent, no one moved.” Fix: “The wind fell silent; no one moved.”
⚡ PART 2 · YOUR TURN

Read each line and name the problem.

⚡ TRY IT · NAME THE PROBLEM
Which problem does each sentence have?
1. “The river glowed faintly, the travelers stopped to watch.”
2. “Moving slowly across the pale grass toward the water.”
3. “He raised his hand the creature went still.”
4. “Because the light was strange and low.”
▶  Now edit. Read your own draft aloud and find one run-on, fragment, or comma splice. Fix it, then click to check your fix.
A clean fix uses a period, a semicolon, a joining word, or an adverb clause. Reread your fixed sentence aloud once more; if your voice flows from start to finish, it is fixed.
💪 BEFORE YOU GO

Editing is care for your reader. Make one slow pass through your whole draft, reading aloud, and fix every run-on, fragment, and splice you hear. Memory work: rehearse both passages this week; the graded recitation comes in Module 3.
