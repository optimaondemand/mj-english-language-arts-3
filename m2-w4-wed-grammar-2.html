To Do, To Be, To Know
Grammar Lesson
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
The word to plus a verb makes a flexible little phrase.
An infinitive is the word to followed by a verb: to explore, to learn, to understand. An infinitive phrase is the infinitive plus the words that finish it: to understand the seroni. Unlike a normal verb, an infinitive phrase does not tell you the main action of the sentence; instead it acts as a noun, an adjective, or an adverb. Ransom wanted to understand the seroni: the phrase names what Ransom wanted, so it works as a noun. Watch for the little word to followed by a verb, and you have found one.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to spot an infinitive phrase (to + verb) in a sentence.
2
I will be able to tell an infinitive (to + verb) from a prepositional phrase (to + noun).
📋 WHAT YOU NEED FOR THIS LESSON
▶
Your writing notebook and a pencil. About 5 to 10 minutes of focused practice.
📚 PART 1 · A WORKED EXAMPLE
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
In which one does to point toward a verb (an action), rather than toward a place? (No wrong answer.)

Here is the test: look at the word right after to. If it is a verb, you have an infinitive. If it is a noun or a place, you have a prepositional phrase instead.

“Ransom longed to see the Oyarsa.” → to + the verb “see.” Infinitive phrase.
“The pfifltriggi worked to carve the stone.” → to + the verb “carve”; it tells why they worked. Infinitive phrase.
“Ransom walked to the water.” → to + the noun “water.” Prepositional phrase, not an infinitive.
⚡ PART 2 · YOUR TURN

In each sentence, is the bold phrase an infinitive phrase (to + verb), or a prepositional phrase (to + a noun or place)?

⚡ TRY IT · INFINITIVE OR PREPOSITION?
Choose what the bold phrase is.
1. Ransom was summoned to meet the Oyarsa.
2. The path led down to the great valley.
3. Ransom tried to explain his home planet.
4. The two races worked hard to understand each other.
▶  In your notebook, write one sentence that uses an infinitive phrase to tell why a character acts. Then click to see an example.
“Ransom stayed silent to avoid offending the sorn.” The phrase “to avoid offending the sorn” tells why he stayed silent. Your own sentence will differ; check that the word right after “to” is a verb.
💪 BEFORE YOU GO

An infinitive is to plus a verb; a prepositional phrase is to plus a place or thing. The word after to tells you which. Memory work: keep working on Drill passage 1.
