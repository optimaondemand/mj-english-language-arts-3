Four Stanzas, One Pattern
Grammar Lesson
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A poem's shape is built out of stanzas, and a stanza's shape often comes from rhyme.
A stanza is a group of lines set off together, the way a paragraph groups sentences. “Invictus,” by William Ernest Henley, has four stanzas of four lines each. Every stanza follows the same rhyme pattern: line 1 rhymes with line 3, and line 2 rhymes with line 4. Repeated four times, the shape gives the poem a steady, marching feel that matches its argument.
🎯 OBJECTIVES
1
I will be able to tell where one stanza ends and the next begins.
2
I will be able to label a stanza's rhyme pattern using letters (A, B, A, B).
📋 MATERIALS
▶
Writing notebook and pencil.
📚 PART 1 · A WORKED EXAMPLE
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
Have you ever noticed a song where the last word of every other line rhymes? What did that pattern make the song feel like?

Here is the poem's first stanza, with the rhyming end-words marked:

Out of the night that covers me, (A)
    Black as the pit from pole to pole, (B)
I thank whatever gods may be (A)
    For my unconquerable soul. (B)

Line 1 rhymes with line 3 (“me” / “be”), and line 2 rhymes with line 4 (“pole” / “soul”). We label that pattern ABAB. All four stanzas in this poem repeat it.

⚡ PART 2 · YOUR TURN

Here is the poem's second stanza. Answer each question about its rhyme pattern.

In the fell clutch of circumstance
    I have not winced nor cried aloud.
Under the bludgeonings of chance
    My head is bloody, but unbowed.
⚡ TRY IT · NAME THE PATTERN
Use the stanza above to answer.
1. Which word rhymes with “circumstance”?
2. What is this stanza's rhyme pattern?
3. What do we call this whole four-line group?
▶  In your notebook, write your own two rhyming lines using an ABAB-style end-rhyme (just the A rhyme is enough for now). Click to see an example.
Example: “The wind came hard across the bay, / and would not let the small boat rest.” (“bay” sets up the A rhyme you would complete two lines later.) Own lines will differ; the goal is to build toward a rhyme on purpose.
