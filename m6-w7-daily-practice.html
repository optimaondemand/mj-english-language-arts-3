Objection!: allusion
A phrase that points to a story
⚖️ THE RULE
An allusion points to a well-known person, place, or story and leans on its meaning. A plain statement carries all of its meaning itself.
OBJECTION! · ALLUSION · NOT GRADED
Testimony: “His first day as a lawyer was his Waterloo.” The witness says this points to a famous defeat, so it is an allusion. Ruling?
Testimony: “She waited for the bus.” The witness says this is an allusion because it names a vehicle. Ruling?
Which sentence leans on a known story?

7
Objection!: irony
Three ways the truth bends
⚖️ THE RULE
Dramatic irony: the reader knows what a character does not. Situational irony: the outcome is the opposite of what was expected. Verbal irony: a speaker says the opposite of what is meant.
OBJECTION! · IRONY · NOT GRADED
Testimony: “Lovely weather,” he grumbled in the middle of a storm. The witness says this is situational irony. Ruling?
The fire station burned down while the firefighters were out. Which kind of irony is it?
Testimony: a girl hides a gift in a closet, and the reader knows her friend is about to open it. The friend does not know. The witness says this is dramatic irony. Ruling?
