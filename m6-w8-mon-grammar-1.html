Craft Lesson
⚒️ TWO TOOLS, ONE JOB: MAKE AN IDEA VIVID
METAPHOR
Says one thing IS another. No like or as.
"In that courtroom, the truth was a locked door no one would open."
SIMILE
Compares with like or as.
"The verdict landed like a stone dropped in a still pond."
Both turn an idea into a picture a reader can see. That picture is what makes an argument stick.
📖 FOCUS
An argument that gives the reader a picture is harder to forget.
A Final Essay or Creative Scene works best when a reader feels the claim, not just nods at it. Two tools do that quietly and well. A metaphor says one thing is another, claiming an identity ("conscience is a debt that comes due"). A simile compares two things using like or as ("the town went quiet, as if it had agreed not to remember"). Each one turns an abstract idea into something a reader can picture. Used with restraint, figurative language makes a point land. Used carelessly, it just decorates. The skill is spotting the difference between a metaphor, a simile, and a plain statement, and choosing the one that serves the point.
🎯 OBJECTIVES
1
I will be able to define metaphor and simile and tell them apart from a plain statement.
2
I will be able to write a metaphor or simile that makes a point in my own essay vivid.
📋 MATERIALS
▶
Writing notebook and pencil. About 5 to 10 minutes.
📚 PART 1 · A WORKED EXAMPLE
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
Think of a comparison that made an idea click for you, in a song, a speech, or a book. Why did the picture stick?

Here is the test. Ask two questions. Does the sentence claim one thing IS another, with no like or as? Then it is a metaphor. Does it compare using like or as? Then it is a simile. If it just reports a fact with no comparison at all, it is a plain statement. These examples were written for this lesson.

"Maycomb's silence after the trial was a wall built overnight." → the silence IS a wall; no like or as. Metaphor.
"The news moved through the town like water finding every low place." → compares using like. Simile.
"After the verdict, the town went back to its ordinary routine." → a plain report, no comparison. Plain statement.
⚡ PART 2 · PRACTICE

Each sentence below was written for this lesson. Is it a metaphor, a simile, or a plain statement?

⚡ TRY IT · METAPHOR, SIMILE, OR PLAIN?
Choose the best label for each sentence.
1. His conscience burned through the long night like a lamp no one could put out.
2. In Maycomb, justice was a slow river that had not yet reached the town.
3. Atticus walked home after the verdict and ate supper with his children.
4. The courtroom held still as a breath that no one dared let out.
▶  In your notebook, write one metaphor and one simile that each make a point you might argue in your Final Essay. Then click to see an example of each.
Metaphor: "Courage in Maycomb was a debt Atticus paid in full while others looked away." Simile: "The town's old habits closed back over the trial like grass over a footprint." Yours will differ; check that the metaphor claims an identity with no comparison word, and the simile uses like or as.
