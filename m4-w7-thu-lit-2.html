Finishing the Island as Evidence
Literature Lesson
📖 FOCUS
A place can make an argument.
Across the module the island has had three settings: the Beach where order begins, Castle Rock where fear takes over, and Simon’s clearing where one boy sees clearly and pays for it. Each can serve as evidence in the essay. Evidence has to be specific. “A beach” proves nothing; a specific detail, such as the conch held at the center of the assembly, can prove a claim about order. For each setting, decide the one claim it supports and identify the detail in the text that shows that claim plainly.
🎯 LESSON OBJECTIVES
1
I will be able to identify the detail in each setting that supports one claim.
2
I will be able to describe the settings in writing as evidence in an essay.
📋 MATERIALS
▶
Lord of the Flies (own copy) and the essay draft.
🧭 PART 1 · REVIEW EACH SETTING AS EVIDENCE
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
As Golding describes Castle Rock, what should the place make a reader feel?

Check each setting against this list. A setting is ready as evidence when a reader could guess its claim before it is explained.

The Beach: Where does the conch appear, and how does the space show open, shared, fair rule?
Castle Rock: Which details show a place guarded, high, and ruled by force? Where do fear or threat show?
Simon’s clearing: Which details set it apart and quiet, a place of insight away from the crowd? How is it both peaceful and exposed?

Use only details that serve the claim. Specific evidence beats crowded evidence.

⚡ PART 2 · WHICH SETTING PROVES WHICH CLAIM?

Match each claim to the setting that best serves as its evidence.

⚡ TRY IT · MATCH CLAIM TO SCENE
Which setting best supports each claim?
1. Claim: the boys begin with the will to govern themselves.
2. Claim: when fear rules, the worse part takes over.
3. Claim: the better part survives only in the one who steps apart.
📝 PART 3 · WORD STUDY

A place can embody an idea. Tap to check the meaning.

⚡ TAP TO REVEAL
embody
Embody means to give an idea a visible, physical form. Castle Rock does not just mention tyranny; it embodies it, so a reader can feel the idea in the space.
🌱 WORD ROOTS
Embody is built from an affix rather than a root. The prefix em- is a form of Latin in-, “into,” and it turns a noun into the act of putting something into it. The same prefix works in embrace, enclose, and entangle.
📝 WORD STUDY · PRACTICE
Write a sentence about how one of the settings embodies an idea. Then rewrite it twice: once with a synonym, once expanding it (“embodies it by…”). Look embody up in a dictionary and record four things: how the word breaks into syllables, how it is said aloud, what part of speech it is, and where the word came from.

Rehearse the passage to be recorded this week.
