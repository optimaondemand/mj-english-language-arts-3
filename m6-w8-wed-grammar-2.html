⏱️ PACING: SENTENCE LENGTH SETS THE SPEED
FAST PACING · short, clipped lines
He ran. The gate slammed. No one followed.
Short sentences hurry the reader along.
SLOW PACING · long, flowing lines
He walked the length of the porch slowly, listening to the boards creak under each step, letting the evening settle before he spoke.
Long sentences slow the reader down.
🎭 TONE SHIFT: the writer's attitude can turn
"It was a fine, easy afternoon…"
→
"…until the room went cold and still."
The attitude turns from warm to uneasy in a single sentence. That turn is a tone shift.
📖 FOCUS
How fast a passage moves, and how it feels, are choices the writer makes.
Tone is the writer's attitude as it comes through in the words: warm, uneasy, bitter, tender. Pacing is how fast or slow a passage moves, and it is controlled mostly by sentence length and the amount of detail. Short, clipped sentences speed things up. Long, flowing sentences slow them down. A tone shift is a deliberate turn in attitude, often used to land a moment. In the aftermath chapters, Lee lets ordinary days move at an easy pace, then tightens her sentences and cools her tone as the story bends toward its final night. Noticing these choices makes it possible to make them on purpose in an essay or scene.
🎯 OBJECTIVES
1
I will be able to define tone and pacing and explain how sentence length controls speed.
2
I will be able to rewrite a sentence to change its pace or shift its tone on purpose.
📋 MATERIALS
▶
Writing notebook and pencil. About 5 to 10 minutes.
📚 PART 1 · A WORKED EXAMPLE
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
Think of a scene in a book or film that suddenly felt faster, or where the mood turned in an instant. What gave you that feeling?

Here is the test. For pacing, ask whether the sentences are short and clipped (fast) or long and flowing (slow). For tone, ask what attitude the words carry, and whether it stays steady or turns. These examples were written for this lesson.

"The door opened. Footsteps. A shout. Then nothing." → short, clipped sentences. Fast pacing.
"The long Sunday afternoon stretched on, warm and slow, with nothing to do but watch the dust drift through the doorway." → one long, unhurried sentence. Slow pacing.
"The picnic was bright and easy, right up until the moment the gate creaked open behind them." → warm turns wary within one line. Tone shift.
⚡ PART 2 · PRACTICE

Each line below was written for this lesson. Read it, then answer the question beside it.

⚡ TRY IT · PACE AND TONE
Answer the question for each line.
1. "She turned. She listened. She ran." Does this speed the pace up or slow it down?
2. "He sat on the steps a long while, watching the light go soft and gold over the rooftops, in no hurry at all." Does this speed the pace up or slow it down?
3. "The street was friendly and familiar, until a cold quiet settled over the porch." What does this line do with tone?
4. "She tucked the blanket around him and left the lamp burning low." What tone does this line carry?
▶  In your notebook, write one calm sentence. Then rewrite it twice: once to speed the pace up, once to shift the tone from calm to uneasy. Then click to see an example.
Original: "The children walked home together along the quiet road." Faster: "They hurried. The road was empty. No one spoke." Tone shift: "The road was quiet and ordinary, until the dark between the trees seemed to lean a little closer." Yours will differ; check that the faster version uses short sentences and the tone shift turns the attitude.
