📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A character is seen most clearly when someone unlike him stands nearby.
A foil is a character set beside another to sharpen the contrast. Golding builds two foils into the heart of his story. Ralph wants order, the rescue fire, and fair meetings; Jack wants the hunt, the thrill, and power over others. Set side by side, each shows what the other lacks. The second pair is quieter but darker. Piggy stands for reason, rules, and the protection of the weak; Roger stands for cruelty that has slipped its leash. As civilization falls away in these chapters, the gap inside each pair widens.
🎯 LESSON OBJECTIVES
1
I will be able to describe how Ralph and Jack work as foils.
2
I will be able to explain what the Piggy and Roger contrast reveals.
📋 MATERIALS
▶
Your own copy of Lord of the Flies, Chapters 7 to 8, and your writing notebook.
📖 PART 1 · LOOK AGAIN AT THE PAIRS
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
Think of two people you know who are opposites. How does each one make you notice something about the other?

Skim back through Chapters 7 and 8 in your own copy. Find one moment that shows Ralph and Jack pulling apart, and one that shows the difference between Piggy and Roger.

A small but telling contrast: when the fire matters most, Ralph keeps asking about rescue while Jack answers with the hunt (Ch. 7–8). Find a line where their priorities collide and mark it in your own copy.

⚡ PART 2 · WHAT DOES THE CONTRAST REVEAL?

For each pairing, choose what the contrast brings out.

⚡ TRY IT · READ THE FOILS
What does each contrast reveal?
1. Ralph guards the rescue fire while Jack leads the boys off to hunt.
2. Piggy argues for the rules; Roger throws stones to frighten the small ones.
3. The boys are tempted to leave Ralph’s camp for Jack’s feast of roasted pig.
4. Roger aims his stones to miss, held back, for now, by a habit of rules he is starting to forget.
📝 PART 3 · WORD STUDY

A foil works by setting one thing in contrast with another. Tap to check the word.

⚡ TAP TO REVEAL
contrast
Contrast means a striking difference between two things placed side by side. As a verb it means to set things together so the difference shows. A foil exists to create contrast.
🌱 WORD ROOTS
Contrast comes from Latin contra, “against,” and stare, “to stand.” Two things in contrast stand against each other. The same stare stands in statue, stable, and circumstance.
📝 WORD STUDY · PRACTICE
Write a sentence that uses contrast to compare two characters. Then rewrite it twice: once with a synonym, once expanding it (“the contrast between… shows…”). Look contrast up in a dictionary and record three things: how the word breaks into syllables, how it is said aloud, and what part of speech it is.
📝 MEMORY WORK

Drill all three passages and say the conch passage aloud once more.
