📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Good writing is rewriting. The draft proves you can think; the edit proves you can be read.
A draft of the Final Essay becomes a finished piece through editing. Editing is not just fixing typos; it is reading one’s own work as a stranger would and asking three questions. First, is every sentence clear: can a reader follow it on the first pass? Second, is the voice right: is active voice chosen where responsibility matters, and are sentences balanced with parallelism? Third, is it correct: grammar, capitalization, punctuation, and spelling? All three skills were practiced this module; here they are applied to the essay. Work through the checklist below, then make the changes in the document.

📚 PART 1 · READ THE DRAFT COLD
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
What is the best first move when you sit down to edit a draft?

The editor’s checklist for this essay. Run the draft against all three layers.

Clarity: Could a reader state the thesis after one read? Does each paragraph make one point?
Voice: Is active voice used where responsibility matters? Are lists and comparisons parallel?
Correctness: Grammar, capitalization, punctuation, spelling. Are quotations copied exactly from the book?

Confirm the essay states and answers at least two strong objections. The rubric scores it.
