Mrs. Dubose and What Real Courage Costs
Literature Lesson
🎯 ATTICUS’S DEFINITION OF COURAGE
NOT THIS
Physical strength, or a weapon in hand
→
BUT THIS
Holding to what is right even when you expect to lose, and carrying it through to the end
Atticus points the children to Mrs. Dubose as his real example of courage, not to himself with a rifle. This is the definition the whole novel tests.
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
The bravest act in these chapters belongs to the least likable person.
Mrs. Dubose is a cruel old neighbor who insults the children and says hateful things about their father. When Jem loses his temper and ruins her flowers, Atticus makes him read to her every afternoon as a punishment. Only after she dies does Atticus tell the children the truth: Mrs. Dubose was determined to break free of a long addiction before she died, and Jem’s reading was helping her hold on through the worst of it. She knew she would suffer; she chose to fight anyway. Atticus calls this real courage, and he sets it deliberately against the easy bravery the town cheered last chapter. Lee builds the moral argument of the whole novel here. The courage that matters is not a clean public victory; it is the private decision to do the right and painful thing even when a loss is expected.
🎯 OBJECTIVES
1
I will be able to explain why Atticus calls Mrs. Dubose his example of real courage.
2
I will be able to connect Atticus’s definition of courage to the central question of the module.

⚡ PART 2 · HOW MUCH DOES IT COST?

Atticus measures courage by what an act costs the person who does it. For each act below, place it on the scale from "costs little" to "costs a great deal," then read why.

⚡ TRY IT · WHAT DOES THE ACT COST?
For each act, choose where it falls.
1. Mrs. Dubose chooses to break her addiction before she dies, knowing it will be agony.
2. Atticus fires one clean shot at the mad dog and the town admires him.
3. Jem returns each afternoon to read aloud to a woman who frightens and insults him.
▶  In your notebook, write two or three sentences answering: how does Atticus’s definition of courage answer this module’s central question about what justice asks of the few who serve it? Then click for one way to connect them.
One connection: Atticus teaches that courage means doing the right thing even when you expect to lose. The central question asks what serving justice costs the few. Mrs. Dubose is a small, private rehearsal for what Atticus is about to do in public at the trial: take on a fight he is likely to lose, because it is right. Answers will differ; aim to link the cost of courage to the cost of justice.
📝 PART 3 · WORD STUDY

Mrs. Dubose holds on to her purpose with quiet tenacity. Tap to check the meaning, then practice the word several ways.

⚡ TAP TO REVEAL
tenacity
Tenacity is the refusal to let go: holding on to a purpose through pain and delay, long after releasing it would be easier. Mrs. Dubose fights her addiction with tenacity, and Atticus wants his children to see that, rather than a gun, as the real shape of courage.
🌱 WORD ROOTS
Tenacity comes from Latin tenere, “to hold.” The same grip holds in tenant (one who holds a property), retain, and tenacious.
📝 WORD STUDY
Write a sentence using tenacity. Then rewrite it twice: once with a synonym, once expanding it ("a tenacity that…"). Look tenacity up in a dictionary and record three things: how the word breaks into syllables, how it is said aloud, and what part of speech it is.
