How a House Becomes a Presence
Literature Lesson
HOW THE RADLEY PLACE GETS ITS POWER
WHAT THE TEXT ACTUALLY SHOWS
A closed-up house. Drawn shades. A family that keeps to itself. A man no one sees come out. Small objects appearing in a tree. Plain, observable facts.
WHAT RUMOR AND IMAGINATION ADD
A monster who roams at night, who eats raw animals, who waits at the window. A whole frightening character built on top of a handful of facts.
Lee lets the town and the children build a presence far larger than anything the facts can prove.
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A place can loom over a whole town long before anyone proves a single thing about it.
Lee turns the Radley place into a presence using the two tools of the week: setting as character and concrete sensory detail. She gives only a few hard facts: a shut-up house, drawn shades, a family that keeps to itself, a man rarely seen. Rumor and the children's imagination then pile a whole monster on top of those facts. The house becomes something that watches, threatens, and shapes how every child in Maycomb moves down the street. The fear is real even though the evidence is thin. That is setting as character at full power: the place acts on the people, and most of what it "does" is built out of what the town and the children invent about it. The task is to separate what the text actually shows from what the characters add.
🎯 OBJECTIVES
1
I will be able to separate what the text actually shows about the Radley place from what rumor and the children invent.
2
I will be able to explain how setting as character and sensory detail make the house a presence in the town.
📋 MATERIALS
▶
Own copy of To Kill a Mockingbird and a writing notebook.
📖 PART 1 · THE FACTS AND THE STORY ON TOP OF THEM
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
Think of a rumor that once spread at a school or in a neighborhood. How big did it grow compared with the small fact it started from?

Skim back through the Radley scenes in Chapters 4 to 6. In your notebook, make two short columns: things the text actually shows about the house, and things the children or the town only imagine or repeat.

📖 READ CLOSELY
Work from your own copy of To Kill a Mockingbird. The text is under copyright, so it is not reproduced here. When you point to a moment, name the chapter and put any borrowed words in quotation marks.
⚡ PART 2 · SHOWN, OR INVENTED?

For each item, decide whether it is something the text actually shows about the Radley place, or something the children and the town invent.

⚡ TRY IT · WHAT THE TEXT SHOWS VS. WHAT THEY INVENT
Shown by the text, or invented by the characters?
1. The Radley house stays closed up, with the shades drawn.
2. The figure inside slips out at night and prowls the neighborhood.
3. The Radley man is a fearsome creature who would harm any child who came near.
4. Small objects begin appearing in the knothole of the oak by the Radley yard.
📝 PART 3 · WORD STUDY

The Radleys keep to themselves and rarely speak to anyone; the town would call them taciturn. Tap to check the meaning, then practice the word several ways.

⚡ TAP TO REVEAL
taciturn
Taciturn describes a person who says very little and keeps to themselves. The Radleys are taciturn, and their silence leaves a gap that the town is only too willing to fill with rumor.
🌱 WORD ROOTS
Taciturn comes from Latin tacere, “to be silent.” The same silence holds in tacit, an agreement understood without being spoken aloud.
📝 WORD STUDY
Write a sentence using taciturn. Then rewrite it twice: once with a synonym, once expanding it ("a taciturn family that…"). Look taciturn up in a dictionary and record three things: how the word breaks into syllables, how it is said aloud, and what part of speech it is.
