Four boys, four ideas
Who carries what
🎭 THE RULE
If the island is a small model of society, each boy can be read as an idea. These are not locked labels. Each boy is still a person.
FEED THE FIRE · NOT GRADED
1. A boy calls a meeting and holds up the conch so each speaker can be heard. Which idea does he carry?
2. A boy cares most about the hunt and about power taken by force. Which idea does he carry?
3. A boy plans and reasons, and his glasses make fire. Which idea does he carry?
4. A quiet boy understands something the others cannot. Which idea does he carry?

Signal Fire Sentences: allegory
A second story under the first
🔥 THE RULE
An allegory is a whole story whose characters and events stand for larger ideas, all the way through. It is not a code with one locked answer.
FEED THE FIRE · NOT GRADED
1. A story has travelers named Greed, Doubt, and Mercy, and their choices play out their names. Is it likely an allegory?
2. A family story includes one locket that stands for a lost mother, and the rest is an ordinary life. Is it likely an allegory?
3. Why do many readers treat the island as an allegory?
Your allegory sentence
From the lesson A Story That Means a Second Thing: why many readers treat the island as a small model of society.

Signal Fire Sentences: irony
What seems true and what is true
🔥 THE RULE
Dramatic irony: the reader knows something a character does not. Situational irony: what happens is the opposite of what was expected.
🐚 ORDER THE SCENE
Click the events in the order they happen in the parachutist scene.
WEIGH THE GAP · NOT GRADED
1. The reader has seen that the ladder is broken, but the climber trusts it. Which irony is this?
2. A guard hired to stop thieves is the one who steals the key. Which irony is this?
3. The boys fear a monster from outside, but the real danger is the fear and cruelty inside them. Which irony is this?
The irony in the parachutist scene
From the lesson When the Truth Is Not What It Seems: one sentence naming the gap or the reversal.
