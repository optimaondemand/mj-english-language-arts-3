Claim, evidence, warrant
A three-part argument about Piggy’s death
⚖️ THE RULE
The claim is what you assert. The evidence is the fact from the text that supports it. The warrant is the reasoning that explains why the evidence proves the claim. Without the warrant, the reader has to guess.
NAME THE PART · NOT GRADED
1. By the end of Chapter 11, the boys settle things with force, not with talk.
2. Piggy dies at the same moment the conch breaks.
3. Since the conch gave the right to speak, its breaking shows that talk no longer rules.
IS IT ARGUABLE · NOT GRADED
Which sentence takes a position someone could argue with?

Signal Fire Sentences: active and passive
Who did it?
🔥 THE RULE
In active voice, the subject does the action: The boys broke the conch. In passive voice, the subject receives it: The conch was broken. The doer can be named at the end or left out. The passive can make an act feel as if no one chose it.
FEED THE FIRE · NOT GRADED
1. The hunters crossed the beach at dusk.
2. The path was cleared before dark.
3. Which version hides who did it?
An active sentence, then a passive rewrite
From the lesson Who Acts, and Who Is Acted Upon. One event from Chapters 10 to 11. Notice what the passive version hides.

Signal Fire Sentences: parallelism
When the parts match
🔥 THE RULE
Equal ideas get the same grammatical shape. In a list, match the form: all nouns, all -ing words, or all clauses that begin the same way.
FEED THE FIRE · NOT GRADED
1. The boys wanted food, to rest, and safety. Which fix makes the parts match?
2. Which sentence is parallel?
Two parallelism fixes
From the lesson When the Parts Match, the Sentence Sings: fix both so the parts match. 2. Their job was to gather wood and keeping watch over the fire. 4. Piggy was brave, loyal, and he thought clearly.
