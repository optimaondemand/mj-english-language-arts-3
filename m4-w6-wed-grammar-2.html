When the Parts Match, the Sentence Sings
Writing Style Lesson
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Ideas that belong together should be built the same way.
Parallelism means giving equal ideas the same grammatical shape. Items in a list or comparison should match in form: all nouns, or all -ing words, or all clauses that begin the same way. Compare these. Not parallel: The boys liked hunting, to swim, and that they could shout. Parallel: The boys liked hunting, swimming, and shouting. The parallel version is easier to follow and lands with more force, because the matching parts make the reader feel the order behind the idea. Writers use parallelism to build rhythm and to drive a point home. It is one of the most useful tools for an argument, because a balanced sentence sounds like a balanced mind.
🎯 LESSON OBJECTIVES
1
I will be able to spot when items in a sentence are not parallel.
2
I will be able to rewrite a sentence so its parts match.
📋 MATERIALS
▶
Writing notebook and pencil. 5 to 10 minutes.
📚 PART 1 · A WORKED EXAMPLE
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
Which one sounds smoother when you say it aloud?

Test: find the items in a list or a comparison, then ask whether they all have the same grammatical shape. If one is an -ing word and another is a full clause, they are not parallel; making them match usually fixes it.

Not parallel: “Ralph valued safety, fairness, and to be rescued.”
The fix: two nouns and an infinitive do not match. Make all three nouns.
Parallel: “Ralph valued safety, fairness, and rescue.” Now the parts match.
⚡ PART 2 · PRACTICE

Each sentence below was written for this lesson. Decide whether it is parallel or needs fixing.

⚡ TRY IT · PARALLEL OR NOT?
Is each sentence parallel?
1. The hunters spent the evening hunting, chanting, and dancing.
2. Their job was to gather wood and keeping watch over the fire.
3. He wanted to be obeyed, he wanted to be feared, and he wanted to be first.
4. Piggy was brave, loyal, and he thought clearly.
▶  In your notebook, fix sentence 2 and sentence 4 so the parts match. Click to see one way to fix each.
Sentence 2: “Their job was to gather wood and to keep watch over the fire.”   Sentence 4: “Piggy was brave, loyal, and clear-headed.” Fixes may differ in wording; the matching items should share the same grammatical form.
