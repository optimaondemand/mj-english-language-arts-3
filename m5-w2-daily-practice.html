🎲 THE RULE
The outline has five lines: the ordinary world, the tradition, the buried hint, the turn, and the blank. Each one is a short phrase, not a paragraph.
WHICH LINE IS THIS · NOT GRADED
1. A bright spring morning; families walk to the lake with folding chairs.
2. Each family sets one lantern afloat, chosen by the oldest neighbor.
3. A girl asks why her lantern is heavier than the others, and her mother changes the subject.
4. No one says why the lanterns are never brought back.

Draw a Slip: start late
Compression and one quiet hint
📜 THE RULE
Start inside the scene and cut the warm-up. Keep almost everything pleasant and bury one small hint. Draw a slip, do what it says, then write your own opening below.
📜 DRAW A SLIP
SLIP 1
Which opening starts in the middle of the action?
SLIP 2
Cut the warm-up. Rewrite this so it starts inside the morning: To understand the market, you first need to know how the town began.
SLIP 3
Which sentence carries exactly one buried hint?
SLIP 4
Write one sentence that opens with a small action already under way. No setup.
SLIP 5
Which detail makes the better single hint?

3
Partner feedback
Trade the draft, then use what you hear
💬 THE TASK
Trade your draft with a partner. Write down what they said about the hint, the turn, and the blank. Then write what you changed. Honest and kind: name what works first.
USE THE FEEDBACK · NOT GRADED
1. Your partner says: “I saw the hint right away.” What should you do?
2. Your partner says: “The end surprised me, but I could find the clue when I looked back.” What should you do?
3. Your partner says: “I still do not know why the custom exists.” What should you do?
What your partner said about the hint
From the lesson Draft It, Then Trade It. Caught too early, or not at all?
What your partner said about the turn
Did it land, or did it come out of nowhere?
What your partner said about the blank
Did something that should stay blank get explained?
What you changed
Name each change you made because of the feedback.
