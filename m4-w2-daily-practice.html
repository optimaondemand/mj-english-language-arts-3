Tone or mood?
The writer’s attitude and the reader’s feeling
🔥 THE RULE
Tone is the writer’s attitude, heard in the words. Mood is the feeling the passage stirs in the reader. Name them separately.
FEED THE FIRE · NOT GRADED
1. The tide slid in, and the sand glittered under a high, bright sun. What mood does it stir?
2. The drums stopped, and no one on the beach dared to speak. What mood does it stir?
3. Which phrase names a tone, the writer’s attitude?
One word from your copywork that carries its tone, and the tone it sets

3
Is it a symbol?
Three tests
🔥 THE RULE
An object is a symbol when the writer returns to it again and again, ties it to a clear idea, and lets its meaning grow.
FEED THE FIRE · NOT GRADED
1. Which object returns through many chapters, tied to the idea of hope and rescue?
2. A boy mentions a leaf once and the book never returns to it. Is the leaf probably a symbol?
3. A boy lets whoever holds the shell speak, and the others agree. Which idea does the shell carry?

Signal Fire Sentences: calm to uneasy
The same island, a different mood
🔥 THE RULE
A writer can change the mood by changing the details. Swap a soft image for a threatening one, and the same place feels different.
FEED THE FIRE · NOT GRADED
1. The embers sank to a dull red, and the dark pressed closer to the shelters. What mood does it stir?
2. Which rewrite makes this calm sentence uneasy? The boys slept under a clear, quiet sky.
3. What does it mean for tone to shift from light to grim across a chapter?
