Objection!: anaphora and parallelism
Repeated words, repeated shape
⚖️ THE RULE
Anaphora repeats the same opening words. Parallelism repeats the same grammatical shape, even when the words differ. A sentence with neither is just linked clauses.
OBJECTION! · NOT GRADED
Witness: “The room was hot, and people were tired.”
Counsel: “Objection. The witness calls this anaphora, but no opening words repeat.”
Witness: “She came early, sat close, and listened hard.”
Counsel: “Objection. The three parts do not share a shape.”
Witness: “Every voice in the square went quiet. Every face turned to the door.”
Counsel: “Objection. No opening words repeat.”
Which revision turns The lawyer stood up, and then a quiet voice was heard, and a question came next. into parallelism?

5
Objection!: antithesis
Two opposite ideas in balanced halves
⚖️ THE RULE
Antithesis sets two opposite ideas in balanced halves. Two details with no opposition between them are not antithesis.
OBJECTION! · NOT GRADED
Witness: “The balcony was full, and the aisle was narrow.”
Counsel: “Objection. This is not antithesis. Nothing here opposes anything.”
Witness: “The crowd wanted a quick end; the truth needed a slow hearing.”
Counsel: “Objection. There is no opposition here.”
Witness: “Some spoke for the truth; others kept silent for safety.”
Counsel: “Objection. The halves do not balance.”
