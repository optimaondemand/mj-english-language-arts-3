Writing Style
PUTTING SPEECH ON THE PAGE
DIALECT (THE SOUND)
A light touch of regional word choice and rhythm suggests a place and a way of life. A few markers do the job; too many turn a person into a cartoon.
Suggest the accent. Do not drown the reader in it.
PUNCTUATION (THE MARKS)
Quotation marks wrap the spoken words. A comma joins the line to its tag, and a new speaker starts a new paragraph.
"I reckon so," she said.
Lee makes Maycomb audible with a few well-chosen markers, never a wall of misspelling.
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
The way people talk can tell you where they are from and who they are, if the writer renders it with care.
Dialogue is the speech of characters. Dialect is the particular form of a language used by a region or a social group: its word choice, grammar, and rhythm. A writer who renders dialect well can make a town feel real on the page. The skill is restraint. A few markers, an unusual word here, a dropped ending there, a phrase no one outside the region would use, are enough to suggest a voice. Pile on misspelled words and the character stops sounding like a person and starts sounding like a joke. Lee gives Maycomb its sound with a light hand. Dialogue also needs correct punctuation: quotation marks go around the spoken words, a comma usually joins the line to its tag ("I reckon so," she said), and each new speaker begins a new paragraph.
🎯 OBJECTIVES
1
I will be able to tell a light, believable use of dialect apart from a heavy one that tips into caricature.
2

📚 PART 1 · A WORKED EXAMPLE
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
Think of a person you know whose way of talking is instantly recognizable. What gives them away?

Here is the test for good dialect: does a light touch suggest the voice, or does heavy misspelling get in the way and turn the speaker into a caricature? And separately, is the dialogue punctuated correctly? These examples were written for this lesson.

"Reckon it'll come a hard frost tonight," he said, eyeing the sky. → one regional word and an easy rhythm suggest the place. Light dialect, done well.
"Ah reckun it'll cum a hahd frawst t'nite," he sed. → every sound respelled, hard to read, the speaker reduced to a joke. Heavy dialect, caricature.
"I suppose it will be cold tonight," he said. → correct punctuation, but no regional flavor at all. Clean, but no sense of place.
