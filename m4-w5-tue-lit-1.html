A Death, Slowed Down, and a Place of Stillness
Literature Lesson
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
When the worst thing happens, the writer's pacing shapes how the reader sees it.
Chapter 9 holds the death of Simon, killed by the other boys in the dark, in their frenzy, while a storm breaks over the island. The horror of the act is real. Yet Golding does something strange and important: just after the killing, his sentences grow long, calm, and almost tender, carrying Simon’s body out to sea under the stars. Reading the passage slowly, one sentence at a time, shows how the rhythm itself shapes feeling. Simon’s clearing is the quiet, green place where, earlier, Simon sat alone and saw clearly. Set against Castle Rock, the clearing argues something on its own: that stillness and insight are the opposite of the mob.
🎯 LESSON OBJECTIVES
1
I will be able to describe how Golding’s sentence rhythm shapes the death scene.
2
I will be able to say what the stillness of Simon’s clearing argues.
📋 MATERIALS
▶
Lord of the Flies, Chapter 9 (own copy) and a writing notebook.
📖 PART 1 · READ, ONE SENTENCE AT A TIME
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
Why might a writer describe a terrible event in slow, beautiful sentences instead of fast, brutal ones?

Read the death scene in Chapter 9 in your own copy, slowly. Read each sentence once for sense, then again for its rhythm. In your notebook, mark where the sentences turn long and calm.

📖 YOUR READING
Read the death scene in Chapter 9 in your copy of Lord of the Flies.

As the tide takes Simon, Golding turns to images of “moonbeam-bodied creatures” and a steady, silver light (Ch. 9). Notice that the language grows gentlest at the moment of greatest loss. Mark that turn in your own copy.

⚡ PART 2 · WHAT IS THE RHYTHM DOING?

Each prompt points to a choice in the passage. Choose the best reading of it.

⚡ TRY IT · READ THE RHYTHM
What effect does each choice create?
1. The killing is told quickly; then the sentences grow long and calm.
2. The water and its small creatures are described with soft, shining, beautiful words.
3. During the killing the boys are a dark, chanting mass, not named individuals.
4. Simon comes down to tell the others the truth, and they kill him as the beast.
📝 PART 3 · SIMON’S CLEARING

The clearing is the quiet place that is everything Castle Rock is not; its stillness makes an argument.

📝 THE SETTING
Simon’s clearing is a small open space ringed by green, with soft light filtering down, a quiet spot to sit, and no path that forces movement in one direction. It is open, calm, and alive, the opposite of bare rock and one narrow way in.
▶  In your notebook, write one sentence naming what the clearing offers that Castle Rock does not. Click for an example.
Example: “In the clearing you can stop, sit, and think; on Castle Rock you can only obey or be punished, so the clearing argues that wisdom needs quiet, not fear.” The sentence should connect the calm of the place to the kind of thinking it allows.
📝 PART 4 · WORD STUDY

Simon’s clearing is a place of serenity. Tap to check the meaning.

⚡ TAP TO REVEAL
serenity
Serenity means a state of calm, untroubled peace. It is the stillness that lets a mind see clearly. The clearing has it; Castle Rock, ruled by fear, never could.
🌱 WORD ROOTS
Serenity comes from Latin serenus, “clear and calm,” used first of weather and open sky. The word still carries that weather in it: a serene person is someone the storm has not reached.
📝 WORD STUDY · PRACTICE
Write a sentence describing the clearing using serenity. Then rewrite it twice: once with a synonym, once expanding it (“a serenity that…”). Look serenity up in a dictionary and record three things: how the word breaks into syllables, how it is said aloud, and what part of speech it is.
📝 MEMORY WORK

Drill passages 1 and 2 and say the death passage aloud once more.
