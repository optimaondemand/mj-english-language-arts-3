THE WORLD OF CHAPTERS 1 TO 3
The Finch home
Scout, her brother Jem, their father Atticus, and Calpurnia who runs the house.
The Radley place
A shut-up house down the street, and the rumors about the man inside it the children call Boo.
The schoolhouse
Scout's hard first day, a new teacher who does not yet know the town, and the Cunninghams and Ewells.
A small Southern town in the 1930s, told by a girl who grew up in it.
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Before you can interpret a story, you have to be sure you followed it.
Chapters 1 to 3 set the whole novel in place. They introduce Scout and Jem, their father Atticus, and the town of Maycomb during the years of the Great Depression; the closed-up Radley house and the figure the children half-fear and half-invent; and Scout's rough first day at school. Lee writes all of it through Scout's eyes, so the facts arrive mixed with a child's misunderstandings. The task is plain: read the chapters in one sitting, then retell the events in order. Interpretation comes later in the week.
🎯 OBJECTIVES
1
I will be able to retell the events of Chapters 1 to 3 in order and in my own words.
2
I will be able to name the main people of Maycomb and how they are connected.
📋 MATERIALS
▶
Own copy of To Kill a Mockingbird and a writing notebook.
📖 PART 1 · READ CHAPTERS 1 TO 3
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
Before you read, picture a small town where everyone knows everyone. What do you expect that closeness to feel like?

Read Chapters 1 to 3 in one sitting, without stopping to take notes.

📖 YOUR READING
Read Chapters 1 to 3 in your own copy of To Kill a Mockingbird. This novel is still under copyright, so the text is not reproduced here.
⚡ PART 2 · PUT THE EVENTS IN ORDER

A plain retell means getting the order right. For each pair, choose what comes first in Chapters 1 to 3.

⚡ TRY IT · WHAT HAPPENS FIRST?
Which event comes earlier in the reading?
1. The children spend a summer fascinated by the Radley house, or Scout starts first grade?
2. Scout learns to read at school, or Scout already knows how to read when she arrives?
3. Scout has a hard day with her teacher, or Atticus talks with Scout about seeing things from another person's view?
▶  In your notebook, retell Chapters 1 to 3 in four or five sentences, in order, in your own words. Then click for a guide to what a strong retell includes.
A strong retell names the setting (Maycomb in the Depression years), the Finch family and Calpurnia, the summer fascination with the shut-up Radley house and the figure the children imagine inside it, Scout's difficult first day at school, and the talk in which Atticus teaches her to consider things from another person's point of view. Keep it plain. Do not interpret yet; just say what happens.
📝 PART 3 · WORD STUDY

Maycomb is a small, almost sealed-off community, and Lee makes its closeness feel insular. Tap to check the meaning, then practice the word several ways.

⚡ TAP TO REVEAL
insular
Insular describes a place or a group that is closed off and inward-looking, cut off from outside ideas. Maycomb is insular: families have known each other for generations, and a stranger or a new idea stands out at once.
🌱 WORD ROOTS
Insular comes from Latin insula, “island.” An insular town lives as though it were an island. The same insula is stranded in isolate and peninsula, literally “almost an island.”
📝 WORD STUDY
Write a sentence using insular. Then rewrite it twice: once with a synonym, once expanding it ("an insular town that…"). Look insular up in a dictionary and record three things: how the word breaks into syllables, how it is said aloud, and what part of speech it is.
